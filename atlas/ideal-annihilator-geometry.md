# Ideal and annihilator geometry of MOD 360

Let

\[
R=\mathbb Z/360\mathbb Z,\qquad 360=2^3\,3^2\,5.
\]

This note identifies the 24 divisor/GCD layers with the full ideal lattice and its annihilator/Fourier duality.

## 1. The 24 ideals

Every ideal of \(R\) is principal and has a unique divisor label:

\[
I_d=(d),\qquad d\mid360.
\]

Hence there are exactly 24 ideals. Their basic sizes are

\[
|I_d|=\frac{360}{d},
\qquad
R/I_d\cong\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z.
\]

Writing

\[
d=2^a3^b5^c,
\qquad
0\le a\le3,\quad0\le b\le2,\quad0\le c\le1,
\]

identifies the divisor/ideal lattice with a \(4\times3\times2\) exponent box. Ideal inclusion reverses divisor inclusion:

\[
I_d\subseteq I_e\iff e\mid d.
\]

## 2. GCD shells are generator shells

Define

\[
C_d=\{x\in R:\gcd(x,360)=d\}.
\]

For every \(x\in R\),

\[
Rx=(\gcd(x,360)).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{C_d=\{x\in R:Rx=I_d\}.}
\]

So the 24 GCD strata are exactly the sets of generators of the 24 ideals. Consequently

\[
|C_d|=\varphi(360/d).
\]

The additive order of every \(x\in C_d\) is

\[
\operatorname{ord}_+(x)=\frac{360}{d},
\]

which is also the size of the principal ideal \(Rx\).

## 3. Element annihilators: the GCD is a cardinality

For \(x\in C_d\),

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(x)
=
\{a\in R:ax=0\}
=
I_{360/d}.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{|\operatorname{Ann}(x)|=d=\gcd(x,360)}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{|Rx|\,|\operatorname{Ann}(x)|=360.}
\]

This gives a ring-theoretic meaning to the ordinary GCD label.

In particular, the 96 prime-capable classes are exactly the classes with trivial annihilator:

\[
\gcd(x,360)=1
\iff
x\in U(360)
\iff
\operatorname{Ann}(x)=0.
\]

## 4. Ideal annihilator duality

For every divisor \(d\mid360\),

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Ann}(I_d)=I_{360/d}.}
\]

The involution

\[
d\longmapsto\frac{360}{d}
\]

is therefore an order-reversing involution of the 24-ideal lattice. In exponent coordinates it is the central reflection

\[
(a,b,c)\longmapsto(3-a,\,2-b,\,1-c).
\]

The 24 ideals form 12 annihilator pairs:

\[
(1,360),(2,180),(3,120),(4,90),(5,72),(6,60),
\]
\[
(8,45),(9,40),(10,36),(12,30),(15,24),(18,20).
\]

There is no self-annihilator ideal because 360 is not a square.

## 5. The same duality is additive Fourier orthogonality

Use the canonical additive character pairing

\[
\langle x,k\rangle
=
\exp(2\pi i xk/360).
\]

The character indexed by \(k\) is trivial on \(I_d\) exactly when

\[
k\in I_{360/d}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{I_d^\perp=I_{360/d}=\operatorname{Ann}(I_d).}
\]

Thus the same complement operation is simultaneously

1. multiplicative annihilator duality in the ring, and
2. additive orthogonal-complement duality in finite Fourier analysis.

The size identity is

\[
|I_d|\,|I_d^\perp|=360.
\]

This is the MOD-360 specialization of standard finite-Frobenius-ring character duality; it is not a novelty claim.

## 6. Ideal arithmetic

For \(d,e\mid360\),

\[
I_d+I_e=I_{\gcd(d,e)},
\]

\[
I_d\cap I_e=I_{\operatorname{lcm}(d,e)},
\]

and

\[
I_dI_e=I_{\gcd(de,360)}.
\]

Annihilator duality exchanges sum and intersection:

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(I_d+I_e)
=
\operatorname{Ann}(I_d)\cap\operatorname{Ann}(I_e),
\]

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(I_d\cap I_e)
=
\operatorname{Ann}(I_d)+\operatorname{Ann}(I_e).
\]

## 7. The eight Boolean/direct-summand ideals

An ideal \(I_d\) is a direct summand of \(R\) iff

\[
\gcd(d,360/d)=1.
\]

The eight such divisors are

\[
1,5,8,9,40,45,72,360.
\]

They are exactly the ideals generated by the eight idempotents:

| \(d\) | idempotent \(e_d\) generating \(I_d\) | annihilator divisor \(360/d\) | complement \(1-e_d\) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 360 | 0 |
| 5 | 145 | 72 | 216 |
| 8 | 136 | 45 | 225 |
| 9 | 81 | 40 | 280 |
| 40 | 280 | 9 | 81 |
| 45 | 225 | 8 | 136 |
| 72 | 216 | 5 | 145 |
| 360 | 0 | 1 | 1 |

Thus the old eight support idempotents are precisely the Boolean part of the 24-ideal lattice.

## 8. Radical and socle

The maximal ideals are

\[
I_2,\ I_3,\ I_5.
\]

Their intersection is the Jacobson radical:

\[
J(R)=I_2\cap I_3\cap I_5=I_{30}.
\]

For this finite commutative ring the Jacobson radical is also the nilradical already found in the atlas.

Its annihilator is

\[
\operatorname{Ann}(J)=I_{12}.
\]

This is the socle of \(R\) as an \(R\)-module:

\[
\operatorname{Soc}(R)=I_{12}.
\]

The maximal/minimal annihilator pairs are

\[
I_2\leftrightarrow I_{180},
\qquad
I_3\leftrightarrow I_{120},
\qquad
I_5\leftrightarrow I_{72}.
\]

The three minimal nonzero ideals have sizes \(2,3,5\), and

\[
I_{180}+I_{120}+I_{72}=I_{12}.
\]

## 9. Pair geometry revisited

For two residue classes \(x,y\), let

\[
t=x-y,\qquad d=\gcd(t,360).
\]

Then simultaneously

\[
Rt=I_d,
\]

\[
|\operatorname{Ann}(t)|=d,
\]

\[
\operatorname{ord}_+(t)=\frac{360}{d}.
\]

Therefore the 24 affine pair-relations previously indexed by \(\gcd(x-y,360)\) are exactly the 24 ideal-generator shells of differences.

This gives the central dictionary

\[
\boxed{
d
\longleftrightarrow
C_d
\longleftrightarrow
I_d
\longleftrightarrow
\operatorname{Ann}(I_d)=I_{360/d}
\longleftrightarrow
I_d^\perp
}
\]

with

\[
|C_d|=\varphi(360/d),\qquad
|I_d|=360/d,\qquad
|\operatorname{Ann}(x)|=d\quad(x\in C_d).
\]

## 10. Interpretation for the atlas

The same divisor \(d\mid360\) now labels:

1. a GCD stratum of points;
2. a unit-multiplication orbit;
3. a principal ideal;
4. the generator shell of that ideal;
5. an affine relation class of ordered pairs via differences;
6. an annihilator size;
7. an additive order \(360/d\);
8. a Fourier-orthogonal ideal \(I_{360/d}\).

This is classical structure assembled into one MOD-360 dictionary. It should be treated as foundational infrastructure for later searches for genuinely non-forced phenomena.
